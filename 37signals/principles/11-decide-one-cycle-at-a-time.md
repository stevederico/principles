# Decide in the room, one cycle ahead, from a few shaped options

**Rule:** Let the people who can say yes choose the next bets in a short meeting over pre-read, shaped options, with no later approval step; commit only the next cycle and bring leftovers back as new pitches.

**Why:** With few options, little time and few people, the meeting steers the product instead of fighting over resources. A later sign-off voids the commitment. Nobody knows what will emerge in six weeks, so staying uncommitted is free. Ask in order: does the problem matter now, is the time right, is the solution worth its hidden costs (a new dependency, screen space), is the timing right, are the right people free.

**Do:**
```
attendees = those with final authority; read proposals beforehand
if "not worth 6 weeks": ask "what if it took 2?" to find the real objection
each bet ends in a working increment; carry nothing over implicitly
roadmap beyond one cycle stays in conversation, not in a plan
```

**Don't:** Design at the table, or commit a multi-cycle roadmap that makes later cycles automatic.

**Break it when:** External dates force sequencing; plan to the date but still make each step shippable. Legal or financial approval happens before the meeting.
